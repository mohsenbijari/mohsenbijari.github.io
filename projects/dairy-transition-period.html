B.Sc. Research Project · 2016

A Study of Methods to Evaluate the Success of the Transition Period in Early Lactation of Dairy Cows

An undergraduate research project focused on evaluating dairy cow transition-period success and its relationship with productive lifespan, physiological performance and longevity-related traits.

← Back to Portfolio

Project Overview

This B.Sc. research project investigated methods for evaluating the success of the transition period in dairy cows during early lactation.

The project examined transition-period success in relation to productive lifespan and considered physiological, performance, welfare and metabolic traits relevant to dairy cow longevity.

Research Focus

  • Evaluation of transition-period success in relation to dairy cow productive lifespan.
  • Analysis of longitudinal physiological and performance data.
  • Investigation of welfare and metabolic traits related to longevity.
  • Interpretation of phenotypic data in the context of dairy cow health and performance.
  • Connecting trait-level analysis with economic and survival-related outcomes.

Methods & Analytical Skills

  • Longitudinal phenotypic data analysis
  • Mixed models
  • Statistical prediction methods
  • Management and interpretation of complex phenotypic datasets
  • Evaluation of relationships between physiological, productive and longevity-related traits

Research Contribution

The project provided an early research experience in quantitative evaluation of dairy cow performance and health-related traits. It also established a connection between transition-period assessment, productive lifespan and longevity-oriented decision making.

This work contributed to the development of an analytical perspective on dairy cattle that later became relevant to broader interests in quantitative genetics, animal breeding, health, reproduction and longevity.
