A Study on some Factors Affecting the Incidence of Foot and Mouth Disease and the Culling Associated with in Iranian Dairy Cows

Mohsen Bijari · Ali Sadeghi-Sefidmazgi

Iranian Journal of Animal Science · Issue 3 · pp. 289–301 · 2023

Abstract

This study investigated the incidence, culling, abortion, stillbirth and weight loss associated with foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) outbreaks in Iranian dairy cows, with a particular focus on interactions between parity and body condition. A total of 49,400 records from 20,330 cows were analysed over the period 2009–2019. The data were collected from nine industrial dairy farms located in several provinces of Iran. FREQ and LOGISTIC procedures in SAS were used to evaluate disease incidence and the effects of FMD on abortion, stillbirth and weight loss.

Key Findings

  • The estimated overall incidence of FMD was 43%.
  • The estimated FMD mortality was 11%.
  • Within-herd FMD incidence ranged from 18% to 59%.
  • The proportion of culling associated with FMD ranged from approximately 1% to 18% across herds.
  • FMD-affected cows showed an average 23% decrease in body condition score (BCS) compared with healthy cows.
  • FMD was associated with an approximately 5% increase in abortion.
  • FMD was associated with an approximately 4% increase in stillbirth.
  • The probability of disease occurrence increased during colder seasons among primiparous cows with abortion and skinny body condition.

Methodology

The study was based on retrospective dairy-cow records collected between 2009 and 2019 from nine industrial dairy farms in Isfahan, Golestan, Fars, South Khorasan, Khorasan Razavi, and Chaharmahal and Bakhtiari provinces. The FREQ procedure in SAS was used to estimate FMD incidence and mortality. Logistic regression using the LOGISTIC procedure in SAS was applied to evaluate the effects of FMD and its interactions with parity and body condition on abortion, stillbirth and weight loss.

Parity & Body Condition

The study identified important interactions between parity and body condition. Among cows with normal and fatty body condition scores, multiparous cows had lower probabilities of abortion, stillbirth and weight loss compared with primiparous cows in the corresponding BCS groups. In contrast, among cows classified as having a skinny BCS, multiparous cows showed a higher probability of weight loss than primiparous cows.

Research Focus

This research focuses on the epidemiological and production-related consequences of FMD in Iranian dairy cattle, particularly the relationships between disease occurrence, parity, body condition, reproductive losses and culling. The findings can contribute to the development of management strategies aimed at controlling and preventing FMD and reducing associated production losses.
