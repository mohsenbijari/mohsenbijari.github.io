Productivity, reproduction and economic losses due to foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) in Iranian Holstein cows

Mohsen Bijari · Ali Sadeghi-Sefidmazgi

Preventive Veterinary Medicine · Volume 238 · 106471 · 2025

Abstract

Foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) is an important infectious disease that can substantially affect the productivity, reproduction and economic performance of dairy cattle. This retrospective study evaluated the effects of FMD on productive and reproductive traits, economic losses and culling trends in Iranian Holstein cows. Data from nine industrial and semi-industrial dairy herds collected between 2009 and 2019 were analysed, comprising 49,400 records from 20,330 cows. Productive and reproductive traits were analysed using mixed linear models, while a simulated bioeconomic model was used to estimate the financial consequences of FMD.

Key Findings

  • FMD was associated with an estimated reduction of 845.9 kg in fat-corrected milk (FCM) per affected cow.
  • Fat yield decreased by approximately 44.1 kg.
  • Protein yield was reduced by approximately 2.3 kg.
  • Calving interval increased by approximately 30.9 days.
  • Days open increased by approximately 13.7 days.
  • The average estimated economic loss was approximately $1,082.3 per FMD case.
  • Culling and replacement accounted for approximately 27% of the estimated economic loss.
  • Reproductive inefficiency accounted for approximately 22% of the estimated loss.

Methodology

The study used retrospective data collected from nine industrial and semi-industrial dairy farms in Iran. Productive and reproductive traits were evaluated using mixed linear modelling. Economic consequences were subsequently estimated using a simulated bioeconomic model incorporating production, reproduction, culling and replacement losses.

Research Focus

This research integrates animal health, quantitative analysis, dairy cattle production and economic modelling to quantify the consequences of FMD at the individual-cow and farm-production level. The study provides an applied framework for evaluating disease-associated production, reproductive and economic losses in dairy cattle systems.
